Skool does not include a built-in "Save Offline" button for most classroom lessons or community call replays, and right-clicking a Skool video does not show **Save video as...**. Lessons are delivered as segmented HLS streams or as embeds from other video hosts, so there is no single media file for your browser to save.

This guide shows how to check whether your creator already enabled downloads, how to save a lesson you have access to as a standard MP4 file, how to identify which player a lesson uses, and how to handle mobile devices or full-course backups.

## Quick Answer: How do I download Skool videos?

Check first whether your course creator enabled a built-in download icon or attached files under the lesson. If not, install the **Video Saver for Skool** extension in Chrome, Edge, Brave, or Opera, open a Skool classroom lesson or community post while logged in, and click **Download**. It detects Skool's native player (Mux/HLS), YouTube, Loom, Vimeo, and Wistia, saving an MP4 locally in your browser.

## Step 0: Check If Your Creator Enabled Downloads

Before installing any third-party tool, check whether the course creator already provides an official download option on the lesson page. Some creators turn on Loom or Vimeo download icons directly inside the player or attach downloadable MP4s, audio files, and slides below the video description, and respecting a creator's settings avoids unnecessary work.

- Look for a download arrow inside an embedded **Loom** or **Vimeo** player toolbar.
- Scroll below the video for creator-uploaded attachments, transcripts, or Google Drive links.
- Check your community's **About** tab or onboarding rules. If a creator has disabled downloads and asks members not to save videos offline, follow those rules or ask the creator before archiving lessons for personal study.

## Step-by-Step: Save Any Skool Video in 4 Steps

Once you have confirmed the lesson has no built-in download button and your community permits personal offline copies, saving a video takes four steps. You install the extension once, open the Skool page you want to save, wait for the player to mount, and choose an output resolution from the Download button.

1. **Install the Chrome extension:** Open the Chrome Web Store and add **Video Saver for Skool** to Chrome, Edge, Brave, or Opera on Windows, macOS, or Linux.

## Which Video Player Is Your Lesson Using?

Skool courses often mix multiple video hosts within the same classroom. Knowing which player powers a lesson helps explain why right-click saving fails and what the extension detects when you open a page. You can spot the host by checking the player controls or branding before you click Download.

| What you see in the player | Player | Supported |

## Why You Cannot Simply Right-Click and Save

A browser can only offer a native **Save video as...** option when the page points directly to a single `.mp4` file. Skool lessons almost never serve video that way; instead, they deliver segmented playlists or third-party iframes that separate video and audio tracks or bind playback to your active Skool session.

| Player | How the video is delivered | What the extension does |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Skool native (Mux/HLS)** | Many small `.m3u8` / `.ts` stream segments instead of one file | Fetches the segments and merges them into one MP4 locally |
| **YouTube** | Embedded iframe with separate video and audio streams | Saves video and audio together as a standard MP4 |
| **Loom** | Embedded player inside the lesson page; check first whether the creator enabled Loom's download icon | Saves the stream your active browser session is playing |
| **Vimeo** | Private or domain-restricted embed tied to Skool | Uses your active Skool session to pick a quality and save |
| **Wistia** | Embedded player with custom player controls | Resolves the best available stream to an MP4 |

Because a single Skool group may host module lessons on Mux, coaching replays on unlisted YouTube, and screen shares on Loom, a tool that handles only one player breaks mid-course. Video Saver for Skool covers all five hosts from one interface.

## Can You Download Videos in the Skool App or Offline?

No. Skool's official iOS and Android mobile apps do not offer an offline video download button, and mobile browsers such as iOS Safari do not run Chrome extensions. To watch Skool lessons offline on a phone or tablet, save the MP4 files on a desktop computer in Chrome, Edge, Brave, or Opera, then transfer them via AirDrop, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or USB.

## Can You Download an Entire Skool Course at Once?

No. Neither Skool nor Video Saver for Skool offers a one-click bulk downloader that grabs an entire classroom automatically. You open each lesson in module order and click **Download** one video at a time, which also keeps requests gentle on your connection and lets you organize files cleanly into numbered folders.

Plan disk space and click-through time before starting a large archive. Standard 1080p H.264 MP4 video typically uses roughly 500 MB to 1.2 GB per hour depending on bitrate and screen motion, so a 10-hour course needs around 5 GB to 12 GB of free drive space. ## Troubleshooting Common Download Issues

Most download issues happen because a lesson uses a lazy-loaded cover thumbnail, a session token expired, or the browser has conflicting extensions active. Checking these four items resolves nearly all missing-button or interrupted-download errors without reinstalling anything.

- **The Download button does not appear:** Click play on the video for a second so the iframe or Mux player mounts. If it still does not appear, refresh the page and confirm the extension is enabled in `chrome://extensions`.
- **A Loom video is private or asks for a password:** The extension cannot bypass passwords or workspace locks. Enter the password shared by your creator inside the lesson player first; once the video plays in your session, see [how to download Loom videos from Skool](/blog/download-loom-videos-from-skool).
- **A download stops partway through:** Signed CDN URLs can expire on long idle tabs. Refresh the lesson page, let the player reload, and click Download again (failed downloads do not count against your 5 free downloads).
- **The lesson is locked or greyed out:** Video Saver for Skool only saves videos your Skool account can already play and cannot bypass paywalls or drip schedules.
