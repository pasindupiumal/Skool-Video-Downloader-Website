Paid Skool communities and cohort programs eventually end. Subscriptions lapse, cohorts wrap up, and once your billing period closes you typically lose access to the classroom modules, coaching replays, and notes you paid to study. If you want a personal offline archive for future review, the only time to build it is while your login still works.

This checklist walks through backing up a Skool course cleanly before access expires, strictly for your own personal offline study—and also helps course creators archiving their own classrooms.

## Quick Answer: How do I save a Skool course before my membership expires?

Start several days before your billing date ends, while your Skool login can still play every video. Install **Video Saver for Skool** in Chrome, Edge, Brave, or Opera, open each **Classroom** lesson and **Community** replay one by one, click **Download** to save numbered MP4 files by module, and save attached PDFs manually.

## What Happens When Access Ends?

Once a Skool subscription expires or a private cohort closes, the classroom tab and feed posts immediately lock behind a paywall or membership check. Because Video Saver for Skool only downloads streams your active browser session can already play, nothing can be retrieved retroactively after your access closes.

| Skool area | While you have access | After paid access ends | Can you archive it? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Classroom video lessons** | Stream in Skool | Typically locked | Yes, one lesson at a time while active |
| **Community call replays and posts** | Viewable in the feed | Typically locked in private/paid groups | Yes, open each post and click Download |
| **Lesson notes, PDFs, and links** | Shown below each video | Typically locked | Save manually: download PDFs, copy links/notes |

**Important:** There is no way to download lessons after your membership ends. The extension uses your active logged-in session and cannot bypass expired memberships or locked tiers.

## Before You Start: Plan the Backup

Backing up a full multi-module curriculum takes disk space and manual click-through time, because lessons are downloaded one at a time rather than in bulk. Spending five minutes checking your renewal date, community rules, and available hard-drive space prevents running out of storage or time on your last evening.

- **Check your end date:** Look up your renewal or expiration date in your Skool membership settings and start at least 3 to 5 days early.
- **Check creator rules and built-in options:** First see if the creator already attached downloadable files or enabled built-in downloads, and confirm your community permits personal offline study copies.
- **Test embedded players while unlocked:** Video Saver for Skool only works on videos actively playing in your logged-in Skool tab; it cannot bypass password walls or restricted embeds. If a lesson includes a creator-shared Loom password in the notes, test unlocking and playing it first.
- **Estimate disk space:** Standard 1080p H.264 MP4 video typically uses roughly **500 MB to 1.2 GB per hour** depending on motion and bitrate, so a 10-hour (30-lesson) course needs around **5 GB to 12 GB** of free drive space.
- **Budget manual time:** Because there is no one-click bulk downloader, set aside time to open and save lessons individually.

## The 5-Step Course Backup Checklist

Follow these five steps in order so every module, video replay, worksheet, and note stays organized and playable offline long after your Skool tab closes. Using numbered prefixes from the start saves hours of renaming scrambled files later.

### Step 1: Count the lessons and choose a plan
Open the **Classroom** tab and count how many videos you want to archive. A few key lessons fit inside the [5 free downloads in total](/blog/download-skool-videos-free) (no credit card required), while backing up an entire curriculum requires unlimited downloads on Standard Monthly or Lifetime Pro.

| Plan | Price | Fits |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Free** | $0 (5 downloads in total, one-time) | Saving a few essential lessons |
| **Standard Monthly** | $25/month, unlimited downloads, cancel anytime | Archiving a full course within one month |
| **Lifetime Pro** | $49 one-time (early-bird pricing; [current price](/#pricing)) | Members backing up multiple communities over time |

All paid plans include a **14-day refund policy**. Technical users comfortable in a terminal who prefer a free, manual command-line approach can also compare [yt-dlp vs a browser extension for Skool videos](/blog/yt-dlp-vs-extension-for-skool-videos).

### Step 2: Set up a clean folder structure
Without leading numbers, operating systems sort video files alphabetically and scramble the module sequence. Create numbered folders with two-digit prefixes before saving your first MP4:

```text
Skool-Course-Name/
├── 01-Foundations/
│   ├── 01-Welcome-and-Setup.mp4
│   ├── 02-Core-Framework.mp4
│   └── 02-Core-Framework-Worksheet.pdf
├── 02-Implementation/
│   ├── 01-Step-by-Step-Walkthrough.mp4
│   └── 02-Case-Study-Breakdown.mp4
└── 03-Coaching-Call-Replays/
    ├── 2026-10-15-Weekly-QA-Call.mp4
    └── 2026-10-22-Hotseat-Review.mp4
```

### Step 3: Download classroom lessons one module at a time
1. Open the first lesson in **Chrome, Edge, Brave, or Opera** on **Windows, macOS, or Linux**.
2. Let the video player load (click play briefly if the lesson displays a cover thumbnail).
3. Click the **Download** button on the video player.
4. Choose a quality and save the MP4 into the matching module folder.
5. Wait for the local conversion to finish, then move to the next lesson.

Video Saver for Skool supports Skool's native player (Mux/HLS), YouTube, Loom, Vimeo, and Wistia from one button, so mixed-host courses stay straightforward. For Loom-specific tips, read [how to download Loom videos from Skool](/blog/download-loom-videos-from-skool).

### Step 4: Do not forget community videos
In many Skool groups, half the value sits in the **Community** feed rather than the Classroom: weekly coaching Q&As, hot-seat reviews, and guest workshops. Filter or search the feed for video posts, open each post so the player mounts, click **Download**, and save the MP4 into your dated replays folder.

### Step 5: Save attachments, then check and back up
- Download attached PDFs, slide decks, and spreadsheets directly from each lesson page, and make your own copies of shared Google Docs or Notion templates.
- Copy written action items and external resource links into a local text or Markdown file inside each module folder.
- Spot-check random MP4 files in VLC or QuickTime to verify video and audio play cleanly.
- Copy the completed archive folder to an external drive or private cloud backup.

## Keep Your Archive Personal

An offline course backup is strictly for your own personal study of material you had legitimate access to while enrolled. Never share saved folders with non-members, upload videos publicly, or resell someone else's course, and always honor community rules; read [is it legal to download Skool videos?](/blog/is-it-legal-to-download-skool-videos) for legal context, Skool's Terms, and DMCA rules.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I download Skool videos after my membership has expired?
No. Video Saver for Skool works inside your active logged-in session and only saves videos you can currently play. Once your access expires and lessons lock, nothing can be downloaded.

### Can I download a whole course with one click?
No. Videos are saved one lesson at a time as you open each page and click Download, so start a few days before your billing date to finish without rushing.

### What if a course mixes Skool videos with YouTube, Loom and Vimeo?
Video Saver for Skool automatically detects Skool's native player (Mux/HLS), YouTube, Loom, Vimeo, and Wistia and saves each as an MP4. It only works on videos actively playing in your logged-in Skool tab and cannot bypass password locks.

### How much disk space will a course need?
Using standard 1080p H.264 MP4 bitrates (~500 MB to 1.2 GB per hour depending on screen motion), a 10-hour or 30-lesson course typically requires around 5 GB to 12 GB of free space. Check available storage before starting.

### Which plan should I choose to archive one course?
Test your setup with the 5 free downloads in total first. If you are backing up one community before canceling, Standard Monthly ($25/month, cancel anytime) is usually the cheapest fit; if you archive multiple communities over several months, Lifetime Pro ($49 one-time) pays for itself in two months.
